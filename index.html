Earth

Why send a signal? What does this experiment teach?

Talking to a spacecraft is like writing a letter: you send it and wait for the reply. The button above lets you feel that wait. The time you wait is the same time space engineers wait every day.

Even light makes you wait

Radio signals travel at the speed of light: about 300,000 km every second. They reach the Moon in 1.3 seconds, but Mars takes between 3 and 22 minutes, depending on where the planets are that day.

Mars can't be driven live

If you spot an obstacle, your "stop" command arrives minutes later. So engineers send the whole day's plan at once. The rover uses its cameras to see obstacles and find its own way.

Nobody can watch a landing live

Landing on Mars takes about seven minutes, but the signal often takes longer than that to reach Earth. So when the team hears "we've started landing," the rover has already touched down.

Lasers at the Moon

Scientists time how long a laser takes to bounce off the Apollo and Lunokhod reflectors. That tells them the Moon's distance to within centimetres. It's how we learned the Moon moves about 3.8 cm farther away each year.

Earth's ears

NASA's Deep Space Network has stations in California, Spain and Australia. As Earth spins, at least one of them can always see the spacecraft.

No answer is an answer too

If the round-trip time passes and nothing comes back, the machine can't hear us or has run out of power. People waited months for Opportunity to answer.

How long does light take?

Bar lengths are logarithmic: each notch means ten times longer. Today's value for Mars comes from a simple orbit model and is approximate.

Podcast series · 5 episodes

The Last
Tuning

The Voyagers’ Golden Record is an instrument that carries music from Earth into deep space, where nobody will play it for thousands of years. Leyla is a made-up instrument with the same fate: an upright piano built in 1923, and her last thirty days before the school closes. The music in the background is made from her own keys, and in every episode a few more keys fall silent. Each episode ends with a real Voyager fact from NASA.
